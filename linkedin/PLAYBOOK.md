## 2. Positioning

**"ACTXION: the voice of the fan, for the people who run the game."**

On X we are the fan arguing in the pub. On LinkedIn we are the company that *listens* to those fans and explains what they think to the industry: clubs, leagues, sponsors, broadcasters and investors.

- Every post answers one question: **"What do fans really think about this, and why should the business of sport care?"**
- Tone: smart, plain-spoken, a bit cheeky, never corporate jargon. Football first, business second.
- The signature series: **"Fan Pulse"** (weekly insight carousel) and **"The fan-side read"** (our take on a sports-business story).

**Who we want to reach**

| Audience | Why they matter | What they want to see |
|---|---|---|
| Club and league commercial, digital and fan-engagement teams | Partnerships, data deals, pilots | What fans think, fan-engagement ideas that work |
| Sponsors and brands in football | Advertising and sponsorship revenue | Fan attention, sentiment, who fans like and dislike |
| Investors and sports-tech VCs | Funding | Traction (500K+), growth, a founder with a clear view |
| Sports-business people and press | Reach, credibility, features | Sharp takes, original data, quotable lines |
| Talent | Hiring | A team that ships and a culture worth joining |

## 4. Formats (what works on LinkedIn in 2026)

| Format | How often | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Document carousel** (6–8 slides, 1080×1350, black, white and ACTXION red, published as a PDF document) | 1 a week (Wed) | Carousels and documents are the highest-engagement format in Metricool's and AuthoredUp's 2026 data, and people save them, which LinkedIn now weighs heavily. |
| **LinkedIn poll** with a business question ("Should clubs give fans a vote on ticket prices?") | 1 a week (Thu) | Polls beat the image and video posts pages usually use (Metricool 2026). It fits "fan verdict". |
| **Text + one image** (take on news, with a Wikimedia HD photo or a branded quote card) | 2 a week (Tue, Fri) | Plain, readable, quick to produce |
| **Milestone or behind-the-scenes post** | when the team has something (aim for 2 a month) | Tells investors and partners the company is moving |

### The look: "the red card" on newsprint

The idea: football's most recognisable object, the referee's red card, in ACTXION red. It carries the one thing that matters on each slide (the number, the verdict, the takeaway). Everything else is printed like a matchday programme or an annual report:
- **Data slides on newsprint** (`#F2EEE5`, black ink); the cover and the closing slide are "night at the stadium". This avoids the near-black-plus-one-accent look every AI tool makes.
- **A relevant real-colour photo on almost every slide:** a photo band across the top of the data slides (the club or stadium in the story), big side-by-side photo tiles for either/or slides, and a full-bleed photo on the cover and closing slide. Photos keep their real colours, never black-and-white or tinted. Pictures make people stop and swipe further, which is what LinkedIn rewards (time spent on the post).
- **Type** is the same as the X images: Anton headlines, Barlow Condensed labels and numbers (stadium signage), Source Serif 4 body (newspaper text).
- **Furniture:** ruled table rows, ballot tick boxes, paper grain, slightly crooked cards. Every data slide carries its source.
- **Templates:** cover, stat, bars, quote, ballot (versus), 3 takeaways, closing slide.

**Sample visuals** (real Deloitte figures, free-licence photo), in the `linkedin-samples` folder:
- `fan-pulse-01.png` … `06.png`: a 6-slide Fan Pulse carousel on the Money League. Cover (the Bernabéu, the €1bn club), ranked bars (Camp Nou), the €12.4bn stat (Allianz Arena), a photo ballot ("Where should the next billion come from?": the Kop vs a fan on a phone), 3 takeaways for clubs (Tottenham Hotspur Stadium), closing slide (Wembley at night). All photos are free-licence and credited on the slide.
- `poll-result.png`: the ballot card for reporting a poll result you send me (Old Trafford). *The ticked result in this sample is illustrative; real cards only show real poll results.*

**How the slides get made and published, automatically:** each routine writes the slides as data, renders them to 1080×1350 images in the cloud (Playwright), saves them to ACTXION's public GitHub repo `actxion-assets`, and gives Metricool the links. Metricool keeps its own copy and publishes the carousel as a swipeable LinkedIn document. Photos: Wikimedia Commons free licences only, credited on the slide.

**LinkedIn polls can't carry images**, so Thursday's poll is a plain LinkedIn poll. Metricool doesn't report poll votes, so results are never guessed: when you tell me the real split, the ballot card can report it.

**Writing rules**
- Line 1 is the hook and must make sense before "…see more" (about 200 characters).
- Short paragraphs, 1–2 lines each. 120–250 words for a text post.
- One clear opinion. End with a question aimed at people in the industry, not "Agree?".
- 3 hashtags at most (#SportsBusiness #FootballBusiness #FanEngagement and similar).
- No "Comment YES", "Like if you agree" or other engagement bait. LinkedIn demoted these in 2026.
- External links: rarely, and never as the whole post. The app link lives on the page's button and in the About section.
- Never invent ACTXION numbers. Only use figures the team has given us (see §8).

### 👤 What only people can do (this is where growth actually comes from)

LinkedIn bans bots and automated activity on personal accounts, so this part can't be automated, and it's the biggest lever.

1. **The founder posts 2× a week from their own profile** (about 15 min a week). Paste from the Monday pack and edit into your own words. Founder posts reach several times more people than the page.
2. **The founder comments on 5 sports-business posts a day** (10 min a day), using the comment ideas from the pack. Thoughtful comments under big accounts get you seen by their audience.
3. **The team reshares each company post with a line of their own** within the first hour (2 min per post). Early comments and reshares tell LinkedIn a post is worth spreading.
4. **Everyone on the team links their job to the ACTXION page** (Experience section → pick the ACTXION page). This puts the logo on their profiles.
5. **Invite connections to follow the page.** The page has a monthly invite allowance under "Grow → Invite connections"; the founder and admins should use all of it every month.
6. **Send the team's real numbers once a month** (downloads, active users, votes cast, top-voted debates). This feeds the milestone posts and the Fan Pulse data. Without it, Fan Pulse can only use public data.
7. **Stop cross-posting football clips to LinkedIn in Metricool** (untick LinkedIn when scheduling the football videos), except one a week with a business caption.

## 8. Guardrails

- **Facts:** one Tier 1 source (BBC, Sky, The Athletic, Reuters, FT, SportBusiness, SportsPro, Sports Business Journal, Front Office Sports, official club, league, UEFA or FIFA sites, Deloitte reports) or two independent sources. Never invent a figure, a quote or an ACTXION metric.
- **ACTXION numbers:** only "500K+ Google Play downloads" until the team sends more. Never imply partnerships, clients or funding that aren't public.
- **No mocking people.** Criticise decisions, not individuals. Nothing on deaths, injuries, abuse or players' families. Anything about legal cases, investigations or a named company in a dispute → held for review in Metricool, not auto-posted.
- **Images:** our own `actxion-slides` renders, plus Wikimedia Commons free licences only (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA), credited on the slide. Never agency photos, TV grabs, club or player images, or AI images of real people. Slides are hosted only in ACTXION's own GitHub repo, never on anonymous file hosts.
- **Automation limits:** routines only *schedule company-page posts* through Metricool, LinkedIn's official partner route. They never like, comment, connect, message, follow or touch any personal profile. The founder pack is drafts only.
- Each run finishes within 40 minutes and retries a failed tool once.
